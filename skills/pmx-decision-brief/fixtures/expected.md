# Decide which integration ships with the launch

## Decision

Which one integration ships with the launch: the design-tool export or the email builder. Engineering needs the answer by Dec 2 to plan the last sprint, and there's room to build one well, not two [C2]. Without a decision by then, the sprint is planned with neither.

## Options

- **Design-tool export first.** One customer's agency rollout waits on it [T4].
- **Email builder first.** Another customer would pilot it in January [T5].
- **Neither at launch.** Ship both later; keeps the sprint for the core product.

## Criteria

1. Commitments at stake: seats waiting or a paid pilot [T4] [T5].
2. Whether it can ship by launch, including partner paperwork [T6].
3. How reversible it is: the other integration can follow after launch.

## Evidence by option

- **Design-tool export.** For: 40 seats wait on it [T4]. Against: the partner paperwork took six weeks last time, which may not fit the date [T6].
- **Email builder.** For: a paid pilot in January if it's there [T5]. Against: *Unknown* how many accounts beyond one need it; sales could find out before Dec 2.
- **Neither.** For: protects the launch sprint [C2]. Against: both commitments wait [T4] [T5].

## Recommendation

The email builder first: it carries a paid commitment [T5] and has no partner paperwork in the way [T6]. The design-tool export follows once its paperwork is under way. If the agency seats turn out to be a signed order, the export wins instead.

**PM decides:** which integration ships with the launch.
